# Content Summary

Both of the resources posted here included a video by Brendan Eich (the author of JavaScript... also I had no idea that he created Firefox and Brave! I use Brave on the daily), while the other included a slick timeline of the development of JavaScript from its 90s inception to its improvement and peripheral spinoff projects over the next 30 years.

## What I Noticed

While I enjoyed listening to Brendan Eich's perspective, I found that his target audience included individuals who knew much more about web development than I... which meant that the conceptual arguments he made were interesting but the more technical details went right over my head. That being said, I did find Deno's introduction extremely helpful because it gave me a broad overview of how the language developed (which I knew virtually nothing about), as well as clarified several key concepts for me. These included the following:

1. The "world" of JavaScript isn't just about the language, it's also directly tied to the browser wars (Netscape and Internet Explorer), has a historical relationship with major other tech groups (MDN, Mozilla, Firefox, Oracle), and is the progenitor of SO MANY spinoff projects that I had only ever heard of before but didn't understand (for example, Node.js).
2. JavaScript has also had a historical rap of being inefficient, slow, problematic, etc... but still has come back as useful, developing, and has gotten more effective faster. For all the gimmicks people had complained about (I never knew how much discourse had been shared about this), it stuck around and got better.
3. I had no idea how much of a relatioinship Java and JavaScript did have (that Sun Microsystems and Netscape collaborated on its announcement) with each other, and with other languages.
4. I had no idea how clueless I truly was about all of this... I've heard Node.js, libuv, Bun, Webpack, Electron, Babel.js, and so many more names mentioned in this article thrown out before, but now I really understand how STARKLY I know absolutely nothing about this material. It's all up from here!
